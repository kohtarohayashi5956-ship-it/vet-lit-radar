Pretreatment with phenoxybenzamine improves short-term survival and hemodynamic stability in dogs undergoing adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma.

Wund Z et al. — J Am Vet Med Assoc 2026

抄録を表示
OBJECTIVE: To evaluate indices of hemodynamic instability (HDI) in dogs and evaluate the effects of phenoxybenzamine (PBZ) on HDI and short-term survival in dogs undergoing adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma. METHODS: A retrospective medical record review of all dogs that underwent adrenalectomy at a single institution between 1996 and 2024. Dogs were included if the mass was confirmed to be a pheochromocytoma on histopathology and excluded if they received α antagonists other than PBZ, had incomplete medical or anesthetic records, or were treated with laparoscopy. RESULTS: 52 dogs met the inclusion criteria. Thirty-five dogs that did not receive PBZ were entered in the NPBZ group, and 17 that received PBZ were entered in the PBZ group. The mean dosage of PBZ was 1.1 mg/kg/d (0.4 to 1.7 mg/kg/d) administered for a mean length of time of 13.4 days (9.3 to 17.4 days) before surgery. Indices of HDI including systolic blood pressure > 200 mm Hg seemed to be more appropriate for dogs with pheochromocytoma. Six of the 22 dogs in the NPBZ group that required cavotomy experienced HDI compared to 0 of 17 in the PBZ group. The 14-day survival rate was 94.1% in the PBZ group and 62.9% in the NPBZ group. CONCLUSIONS: Pharmacologic α-adrenergic blockade with PBZ prior to adrenalectomy improved short-term patient survival. Indices of HDI that include systolic arterial pressure above 200 mm Hg seem to be more appropriate for dogs. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: PBZ should be considered as an effective pretreatment strategy to reduce the effects of HDI during adrenalectomy in dogs undergoing adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma.

Canine gastric polyps are predominantly hyperplastic, and endoscopic polypectomy is a safe and effective treatment associated with no recurrence during follow-up.

Zanardi S et al. — Am J Vet Res 2026

抄録を表示
OBJECTIVE: To characterize the clinical presentation, endoscopic features, and histopathological findings of gastric polyps in dogs and to evaluate the safety and outcomes of endoscopic polypectomy. METHODS: Dogs diagnosed with gastric polypoid lesions during upper gastrointestinal endoscopy from January 2020 through December 2024 at the University Veterinary Hospital, University of Bologna (Italy), were retrospectively evaluated. Inclusion criteria were endoscopic identification of gastric polyps; dogs with primary gastric neoplasia were excluded. Signalment, clinical signs, history of proton pump inhibitor administration, endoscopic findings, histopathology, treatment, complications, and follow-up data were collected. Endoscopic polypectomy was performed using cold biopsy forceps or electrosurgical snare. RESULTS: Over a 5-year period, 57 dogs were included from 574 canine gastroscopies performed, yielding a detection rate of 9.9% (57 of 574). Polyps were solitary in 54.4% and multiple in 45.6% of cases, mainly located in the gastric antrum (77.2%). All polyps were hyperplastic on histopathology. Endoscopic polypectomy was performed in 17 of 57 dogs, with no intraoperative complications. Mild postoperative complications occurred in 5 of 17 dogs. Clinical signs resolved in all but 1 case and remained absent during follow-up (median, 16 months). Polyps removed with electrosurgical snare were significantly larger than those removed with cold biopsy forceps, with an exploratory cutoff of 10 mm distinguishing between the 2 techniques. No recurrences were documented based on clinical follow-up. CONCLUSIONS: Gastric polyps in dogs are uncommon lesions, typically hyperplastic and associated with chronic inflammation. Endoscopic polypectomy appears to be a safe and effective treatment and recurrence-free at follow-up. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Endoscopic polypectomy represents a minimally invasive option for the management of gastric polyps in dogs, allowing clinical resolution with low morbidity in selected cases.

Renoprotective effects of curcumin in cats with chronic kidney disease.

İlgün M et al. — J Vet Intern Med 2026

抄録を表示
BACKGROUND: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) in cats is characterized by oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis. Curcumin has shown renoprotective effects in experimental models, but clinical evidence in cats with CKD remains limited. HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: Evaluate whether PO curcumin supplementation (80 mg/cat/day), administered with standard treatment, favorably modulates renal, oxidative, inflammatory, and apoptotic biomarkers in cats with International Renal Interest Society (IRIS) stage 3 CKD. ANIMALS: Sixteen client-owned cats with IRIS stage 3 CKD. METHODS: Prospective, randomized trial. Cats were allocated to medical treatment alone (MT; n = 8) or medical treatment plus curcumin (MT + C; n = 8). Curcumin was administered PO at 80 mg/cat/day in 2 divided doses for 60 days. RESULTS: Baseline renal biomarkers did not differ between groups. The primary outcome, serum symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA) concentration, was significantly lower in the MT + C group than in the MT group on days 30, 45, and 60 (P < .01). Urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio decreased over time in the MT + C group and was lower than in the MT group at later time points. Total antioxidant status increased, whereas total oxidant status decreased in the MT + C group, with a significant between-group difference in total oxidant status on day 45. Findings for nuclear factor kappa B and caspase-3 were also consistent with decreased inflammatory and apoptotic activity in curcumin-treated cats. CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Curcumin supplementation was associated with improvement in SDMA and favorable changes in mechanistic biomarkers in cats with IRIS stage 3 CKD.

Early activation of Virchow's triad in feline hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: beyond spontaneous echo contrast and insights into translational thromboembolism.

de Sousa FG et al. — Front Vet Sci 2026

抄録を表示
The hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) phenotype is closely associated with arterial thromboembolism (ATE). The pathophysiology of ATE is linked to activation of Virchow's triad, characterized by endothelial injury, blood stasis, and hypercoagulability. Together, these factors create a prothrombotic microenvironment that may precede overt clinical manifestations. Although spontaneous echocardiographic contrast (SEC) is widely used in clinical practice as a marker of thromboembolic risk, it should be regarded as a late finding. This review explores the early activation of Virchow's triad in cats with HCM phenotype, with emphasis on atrial remodeling, hemodynamic alterations, and endothelial dysfunction preceding detectable SEC. Evidence suggests that changes in left atrial appendage dynamics contribute to localized stasis, while systemic mechanisms, including platelet activation, thrombin generation, and inflammatory processes, promote a hypercoagulable state. Additionally, the presence of SEC should be interpreted as a biomarker of blood stasis rather than a causal factor. Reliance on SEC for thrombotic risk assessment reflects limitations in identifying the pre-thrombotic state, particularly due to the lack of standardized biomarkers for routine clinical use. Therefore, improved thromboembolic risk stratification in cats with HCM phenotype is needed. Overall, thrombogenesis in cats with cardiac disease is a dynamic, multifactorial, and progressive process that begins prior to detectable echocardiographic changes. A clearer understanding of these mechanisms may enable earlier identification of high-risk patients and support preventive therapeutic strategies. This review analyzes the early activation of Virchow's triad in cats with HCM phenotype and its relationship with atrial thrombus formation, with emphasis on thrombogenic mechanisms preceding SEC and their implications for ATE.

Clinical use of sodium bicarbonate in small animals: indications, electrolyte changes and survival outcome.

OBJECTIVES: The objective of the study was to describe the use of sodium bicarbonate in dogs and cats. MATERIALS AND METHODS: Retrospective descriptive study of animals administered sodium bicarbonate from 2019 to 2023 in a university teaching hospital. Data collected included clinical use, dose and infusion duration, pre- and post- sodium bicarbonate venous blood gas values and perfusion parameters and outcome. RESULTS: Twenty-five dogs and 28 cats were identified. Kidney disease was the most common indication for sodium bicarbonate administration in dogs and cats. The median dose of sodium bicarbonate administered in dogs and cats was 2.12 mEq/kg (range: 0.68 to 6.48 mEq/kg) and 2.49 mEq/kg (range: 1 to 6.48 mEq/kg), respectively. There was no correlation between sodium bicarbonate dose with change in pH, HCO3, ionised calcium, sodium or potassium. There was no significant association with sodium bicarbonate dose and survival (OR: 0.724, 95% CI 0.410 to 1.277) or pH change and survival (OR: 0.676, 95% CI 0.323 to 1.410). CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: This study provides a description of current indications, dosing and electrolyte changes that may occur in animals administered sodium bicarbonate.

Clinical findings and outcomes in canine ischemic neuromyopathy: neurologic status is associated with survival, and recovery is possible.

Atkins C et al. — Am J Vet Res 2026

抄録を表示
OBJECTIVE: To describe the clinical findings and outcomes in dogs diagnosed with thoracic or pelvic limb ischemic neuromyopathy (INM) in a referral practice. METHODS: This was a retrospective, multicenter study of client-owned dogs diagnosed with thoracic or pelvic limb INM at 5 referral hospitals (July 2013 to June 2023). Medical records were reviewed for clinical findings, comorbidities, treatments, and outcomes. The primary outcome was survival to discharge. Long-term outcomes were summarized when follow-up was available. RESULTS: 42 dogs met the inclusion criteria: 4 of 42 (9.5%) had thoracic limb INM, and 38 of 42 (90.5%) had pelvic limb INM. Comorbidities were documented in 35 of 42 (83.3%), most commonly protein-losing nephropathy (21 of 42 [50.0%]). All thoracic limb cases were ambulatory at presentation but were euthanized shortly after diagnosis at the owner's discretion. Among pelvic limb cases, 22 of 38 (57.9%) were ambulatory at presentation, 27 of 38 (71.1%) retained nociception, and 15 of 38 (39.5%) had onset of clinical signs > 24 hours. Nineteen of 38 (50.0%) survived to discharge. Among dogs with long-term outcome available, 7 of 28 (25.0%) fully recovered, including 2 of 16 (12.5%) initially nonambulatory dogs. CONCLUSIONS: In dogs with pelvic limb INM, ambulatory status, presence of nociception, and more gradual progression to maximal clinical severity were associated with survival to discharge. Complete recovery occurred in a subset of initially nonambulatory dogs, indicating that recovery is possible despite more severe neurologic deficits at presentation. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Prognostic indicators such as ambulatory status and nociception should be used to inform clinical decision-making but should not be used in isolation when considering prognosis, as recovery may occur even in more severely affected dogs.

Diagnostic evaluation of a flow cytometry-based direct antiglobulin test and demographic analysis in dogs with suspected immune thrombocytopenia.

Nedumpun T et al. — Vet J 2026

抄録を表示
Canine immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) is an autoimmune haematological disorder characterized by antibody-mediated platelet destruction, and diagnosis remains challenging because of limited definitive diagnostic tools. This study evaluated an in-house flow cytometry-based direct antiglobulin test (DAT) for the detection of platelet-bound IgG and investigated demographic factors associated with DAT positivity in thrombocytopenic dogs. A total of 100 healthy dogs and 1,127 thrombocytopenic dogs were included. Platelets were stained with anti-CD41/61 and anti-canine IgG antibodies and analysed by flow cytometry. A diagnostic cutoff was established using healthy control dogs and subsequently applied to thrombocytopenic dogs. Breed, age, and sex were evaluated as potential risk factors for DAT positivity. A cutoff value of 10% IgG-bound platelets was established, and 39.3% of thrombocytopenic dogs were classified as DAT-positive. DAT-positive dogs had significantly lower platelet counts than DAT-negative dogs (median, 10,000/µL; mean, 23,665/µL vs. median, 39,000/µL; mean, 53,193/µL; P < 0.05). A weak but statistically significant inverse correlation was observed between the percentage of IgG-bound platelets and platelet count (r = -0.17, P = 0.0062). Mixed-breed and Shih Tzu dogs, young to middle-aged dogs, and intact females had increased odds of being DAT-positive. These findings suggest that integrating flow cytometry-based DAT results with platelet count and demographic characteristics may improve the diagnostic workup for canine ITP and support therapeutic decision-making.

Nephrotomy as a Surgical Approach for Renal Dioctophymosis in Eighteen Dogs.

Silva LF et al. — Vet J 2026

抄録を表示
Dioctophymosis is a zoonotic disease caused by Dioctophyme renale, a nematode that can reach up to 100cm in length and is commonly known as the "giant kidney worm." The parasite shows a marked predilection for the right kidney, and ultrasonography is the diagnostic method of choice, allowing visualization of characteristic tubular structures within the renal parenchyma. Surgical intervention remains the only established treatment, with nephrectomy being the most commonly performed procedure, although nephrotomy may be considered in cases with residual renal parenchyma. This study aimed to retrospectively describe the surgical technique, patient selection criteria, and postoperative outcomes of open nephrotomy in dogs with renal D. renale infection. Eighteen dogs diagnosed with right renal dioctophymosis between 2017 and 2024 were included. Patient selection was based on ultrasonographic evidence of preserved renal parenchyma. A paracostal approach was used in most cases to optimize renal exposure, and vascular occlusion was achieved using a Rummel tourniquet, with ischemia time maintained below 20minutes. Parasite removal was successfully achieved in all patients. Postoperative monitoring showed stable or improved serum creatinine concentrations in the evaluated cases. Nephrotomy was a feasible surgical option in selected patients with renal dioctophymosis. Early diagnosis, before complete parenchymal destruction occurs, may increase the number of candidates for this approach, although further prospective studies are needed to better characterize long-term outcomes and refine patient selection criteria.

The presence of a hypercoagulable state in cats with cardiomyopathies with or without thromboembolic disease: a scoping review.

Livingstone S et al. — Vet J 2026

抄録を表示
Despite many decades of research into the underlying pathophysiology of thromboembolic disease in cats with cardiomyopathy, findings across the literature are still limited with inconsistencies across study findings. The aim of this scoping review is to describe the literature evaluating thromboembolic disease in cats with cardiomyopathy, to identify coagulation pathways that are involved, and their respective findings and limitations. All studies except two had a healthy control cat group for comparison, and intervention groups varied with regards to severity of cardiomyopathy, and/or the presence of thromboembolic disease. Included studies were observational and randomised controlled trials investigating the difference in coagulation abnormalities between cats with cardiomyopathy and healthy cats. Studies were identified using Pubmed and Scopus, from the first paper available until December 2024. The search resulted in 244 studies. Duplicate, title and abstract screening resulted in 29 studies assessed for eligibility; 17 of which were included; of which 15 were observational and 2 were randomised controlled trials. Groups of interest included were cats with cardiomyopathy (hypertrophic, dilated and restrictive cardiomyopathies) that fulfilled the inclusion criteria and explored coagulation activity. The most consistent findings from this scoping review was the presence of increased von Willebrand Factor and platelet activation across the studies. The remaining results across studies were mixed, potentially due to small study populations, uneven spread of cardiomyopathy severity amongst cats with cardiomyopathy, varying subgroupings, and other possible confounding factors. This study has shown that our knowledge of the underlying pathophysiology of thromboembolic disease in cats with cardiomyopathy still has large knowledge gaps with great potential for further discoveries and findings.
